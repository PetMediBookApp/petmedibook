Traveling with your dog? The health documents you need

Whether it's a road trip, a flight, or boarding your dog for a few days, you'll almost always be asked for proof of health and vaccinations. Here's what to prepare — and how to keep it all in your pocket instead of a folder at home.

The documents you'll likely need

Exact requirements depend on your destination and how you travel, so always check the official rules for your route. That said, these come up again and again:

  • Proof of rabies vaccination — the single most commonly required item, often with a minimum time since the shot.
  • Core vaccination history — dates for the main vaccines (e.g. DHPP), useful for boarding and some border crossings.
  • Microchip number — frequently required and often cross-checked against the vaccination record.
  • A recent vet health certificate — some airlines and countries require one issued within a set number of days before travel.
  • Parasite treatment records — tick/tapeworm treatment is required for some destinations within a specific window before entry.

Prepare a week ahead

Many requirements are time-sensitive (a health certificate issued within X days, a treatment given within a specific window). Check the rules at least a week before you leave so there's time to book a vet visit if something is missing or a booster is due.

Keep it handy — not at home

The classic mistake is having everything neatly organized in a drawer that's 300 miles away when you're asked for it. Keep a digital copy on your phone: your pet's vaccination dates, microchip number, and photos of the actual certificates. That way you can show or send proof on the spot — at check-in, the border, or the boarding facility.

One-tap proof saves stress

Being able to pull up a clean summary — or export a PDF — in seconds turns a stressful checkpoint into a non-event. Before you leave, do a final check: rabies in date, microchip noted, certificate photographed, next due dates set for when you're back.
