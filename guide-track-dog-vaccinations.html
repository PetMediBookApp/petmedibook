How to keep track of your dog's vaccinations

Between the paper booklet in a drawer and photos scattered across your phone, it's easy to blank out at the vet when they ask "when was the last vaccination?" Here's a simple, free method that keeps your dog's vaccine history organized and always on hand — even if you switch vets.

1. Gather what you already have

Start by pulling together everything in one session: the paper vaccination booklet, any vet invoices or emails, and photos you've taken of documents. Lay them out and note the essentials for each vaccination:

  • The vaccine name (e.g. rabies, DHPP, kennel cough / Bordetella)
  • The date it was given
  • The due date for the next dose or booster
  • The clinic or vet who administered it

2. Record it in one durable place

Paper booklets fade, get lost, or stay at home exactly when you need them. A photo buried in your camera roll is hard to find under pressure. The goal is a single, durable record you can open in seconds. A dedicated pet health app keeps each vaccination with its date and next-due date, so you always have the answer ready — and you can keep multiple pets separate and tidy.

3. Set reminders for what's next

The point of tracking isn't just history — it's not missing the next dose. Once you've logged the dates, set a reminder for each upcoming booster. A good rule of thumb: add a reminder a couple of weeks before the due date, so you have time to book the appointment.

4. Keep an exportable copy for the vet

When you visit a new clinic, travel, or board your dog, you'll often be asked for proof of vaccination. Being able to export a clean summary (a PDF you can show or send) saves time and avoids unnecessary re-vaccination. Keep this in mind when choosing how to store records: can you get the data back out easily?

5. Do a quick review twice a year

Twice a year, spend five minutes checking that everything is up to date: recent vaccinations logged, next due dates set, weight noted. Small, regular upkeep beats a frantic scramble before a vet visit.
